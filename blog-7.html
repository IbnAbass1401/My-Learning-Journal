Moving From Tutorials to My Own Projects

Learning why building independently has become one of the most important parts of my journey.

One thing I've become more certain about during this journey is that I learn best when I'm building.

Tutorials are useful because they introduce concepts and show how things work, but eventually I need to close the tutorial and figure things out myself.

That's where projects become different.

When I build something on my own, I have to decide how I want the page structured, what elements I need, how I want them styled and what should happen when someone interacts with it.

Sometimes I know exactly what I want to do but don't know the code yet.

Sometimes I write something that doesn't work.

Sometimes I spend a while trying to understand why something isn't behaving the way I expected.

But those moments have become some of the most useful parts of my learning.

I've built small projects such as a car/people counter, a unit converter and other JavaScript exercises while learning.

I've also started working on larger projects where I'm thinking more about design, responsiveness and the overall user experience.

The goal is no longer just:

"Can I complete this tutorial?"

It's becoming:

"Can I build this myself?"

That shift is important to me because eventually I want to work on real products where there isn't going to be a tutorial telling me exactly what to type.
